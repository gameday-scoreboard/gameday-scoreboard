Between games the panel can show what's coming up for your team, and once the season is over, how it went.

Both cards are for football teams (NFL and college) and soccer teams (MLS and the Premier League) in **Favorite teams** mode. Basketball, hockey and baseball teams don't get them yet: ESPN's season schedules for those leagues are too big for the panel to read.

## Turn them on or off

Both are on to start. **Schedule** and **Season recap** are under **Between games** on the [device page](/using/device-page/). Without a football or soccer team in the playlist that's playing, they're grey and say so.

## What they show

Every 5 minutes between games, one of your favorites takes a turn, and the next turn is the next favorite's.

- **Schedule**, during the season: the team's next three games under a line like **DAL SCHEDULE**, each with the day, the date, the opponent (**@** for a road game) and the start time, plus the TV network when there's room. On two panels each game takes one line, like **Sun 11/1 vs ARI 12P Fox**; on one panel, two.
- **Season recap**, between seasons: the season's record under a line like **DAL 2025**, and how the playoffs ended, like **11-6, lost NFC Wild Card** or **15-2, won Super Bowl LX**. Soccer records are wins, draws and losses. A team that missed the playoffs shows its record alone.

A team is between seasons when the panel's [off-season clock](/using/brightness-and-clock/#the-off-season-clock) would come up for it: no game left on its schedule, or for soccer, nothing for more than 10 days, which can include a long international break.

Each card stays up for 10 seconds, longer if its lines roll, then the board comes back.

## Where it comes from

The panel reads each team's season schedule from ESPN: about 300 KB for an NFL team, 100 KB for a college team, 450 KB for an MLS team and 75 KB for a Premier League team. It reads each favorite at most once every 6 hours, and only while one of the two cards is on and it's that team's turn. If a read fails, the panel tries that team again in 15 minutes and goes on to the next one meanwhile.

## When they step aside

Like the [news card](/using/news/), they never cover a live game, a celebration, or the panel's own trouble messages, and they take turns with the message card, the news card, the College Top 25 and your pictures: none of them comes up over another.
