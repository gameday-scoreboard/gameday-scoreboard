Golf is a leaderboard, not a game between two teams, so the panel shows it as one: the current tournament's top ten, with the golfers you follow pinned onto the board wherever they stand. It covers the **PGA Tour**, the **LPGA** and the **DP World Tour**.

## Follow a tour

On the device page's **Display** tab, open **Golf leaderboard** under **Between games** and tap the tours you want: **PGA Tour**, **LPGA**, **DP World Tour**. Each one you pick shows its leaderboard between games. Tap a tour again to stop showing it.

You don't need to follow any golfer for this. The card stays greyed out, with **Pick a tour below, or follow a golfer**, until there's a tour to show.

## Follow a golfer

On the **Teams** tab, open **Add a favorite**, pick the **PGA**, **LPGA** or **DPWT** tab and type part of a name in the search box, like `schef`. The page looks the name up with ESPN's player search and lists the golfers who match, each with their tour. Tap one to follow them, and tap again to stop.

A golfer you follow:

- takes a place in your favorites list, by name, and counts toward the same limit as teams;
- turns on their tour's leaderboard, even if you didn't pick that tour on the card;
- stays on the board while they're in the field, even when they're far down the leaderboard.

A golfer who isn't playing this week simply isn't on the board. They come back the next time they play.

The search needs the phone or computer you're using to reach ESPN. The panel itself only stores each golfer's ESPN number.

## When it shows

The leaderboard shows between games, like the [College Top 25](/using/top-25/): it takes a turn every 10 minutes while no game is on, five seconds a page, and never covers a game in progress, a score splash or your [message card](/using/messages/). With more than one tour, the tours take turns.

While a round is being played, the panel reads the tour's leaderboard every five minutes. Overnight between rounds, or while weather has stopped play, it reads every half hour. Between tournaments it reads every few hours, and at the first tee time once ESPN has posted the next event. A tournament more than four days over is left off.

## What it looks like

The top line names the tour and the round, like **PGA R2** during the second round, or **PGA FINAL** once the tournament is over. A round stopped for weather or dark still shows its round number. Each row has the golfer's position and score:

```
 1 James      -11
 2 Greyserman -10
 3 Fisk       -10
```

A single 64x64 panel shows surnames, five rows a page. A 128x64 display shows one full-width column with the golfer's name, and while a round is being played, today's score and the hole they're through:

```
 1 B. James        -11 -4 9
 2 M. Greyserman   -10 -3 11
```

The board holds the top ten, plus up to six golfers you follow who stand below them, in position order. Scores are shown the way ESPN writes them: under par as a negative, level par as **E**.

Some things the board shows only as ESPN has them:

- **Thru:** only the PGA Tour's leaderboard carries hole-by-hole scores. On the LPGA and DP World Tour the hole a golfer is through is left off, not guessed.
- **Missed cut or withdrawn:** ESPN doesn't mark these. A golfer who missed the cut simply has no weekend rounds.
- **Team events:** in a pairs event like the Zurich Classic, the board lists the pairs, "Smalley/Springer" on a wide board and "Smalley/S" on a single panel.

## Turning it off

Remove **Golf leaderboard** from the **Between games** list to stop it altogether. Golfers you follow stay in your favorites. Tapping off every tour on the card leaves only the tours of the golfers you follow.
