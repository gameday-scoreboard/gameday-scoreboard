Golf is a leaderboard, not a game between two teams, so the panel shows it as one: the current tournament's top scores, with the golfers you follow pinned into the board wherever they stand. It covers the **PGA Tour**, the **LPGA** and the **DP World Tour**.

## Following a golfer

A golf favorite is a golfer, not a team. On the device page's Teams tab, open **Add a favorite** and pick the tour's tab — **PGA**, **LPGA** or **DPWT**. Golfers are followed by their ESPN athlete id, the number at the end of the golfer's page on espn.com (for example `espn.com/golf/player/_/id/5077389`): type the id and tap **Add golfer**. The golfer takes a place in your favorites list like a team does, and counts toward the same limit.

A tour's field changes every week, so there is no list of golfers to pick from, and a golfer who isn't playing this week simply isn't on the board — he comes back on it the next time he plays. Following golfers in more than one tour is fine; the tours take turns.

## When it shows

The leaderboard shows between games, like the [College Top 25](/using/top-25/): it takes a turn every 10 minutes while no game is on, five seconds a page, and never covers a game in progress, a score splash, or your [message card](/using/messages/). A panel follows a tour once you follow a golfer in it.

While a round is being played the panel reads the tour's scoreboard again every five minutes. Between tournaments it reads once a day: the scoreboard names the next event as soon as ESPN posts it, so the board is there when the first round starts. A tournament more than four days over is left off.

## What it looks like

The top line names the tour and the round, like **PGA R2** while the second round is played, or **PGA FINAL** once the tournament is over. Each row has the golfer's position and score:

```
 1 James      -11
 2 Greyserman  -10
 3 Fisk       -10
```

A single 64x64 panel shows surnames, five rows a page. A 128x64 display shows one full-width column with the golfer's name, and while a round is being played, today's score and the hole he is through:

```
 1 B. James        -11 -4 9
 2 M. Greyserman   -10 -3 11
```

The board holds the top ten, plus up to six golfers you follow who stand below them, in position order — a followed golfer is never left off his tour's board while he is in the field. Scores are shown the way ESPN writes them: under par as a negative, level par as **E**. Golfers who miss the cut or withdraw simply have no later rounds; the board shows the rounds the document carries and invents nothing.

Thru counts come from the document's hole-by-hole linescores, which only some tours carry; where ESPN doesn't post them, the thru is left off rather than guessed.

## Turning it off

Turn it off with **Golf leaderboard** under **Between games** on the [device page](/using/device-page/). The golfers stay in your favorites; the card just stops taking turns.
